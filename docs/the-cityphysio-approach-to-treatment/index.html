How we work

We adopt a three-stage, hands-on approach to the management of all conditions. The order matters: nothing is treated before it is understood.

01

Assessment

A detailed history and a physical examination, so we can name the problem.

Your physiotherapist takes a detailed history and carries out a physical examination. The aim is to identify the current pathology, its probable cause, and any factors that may affect your recovery. Afterwards they will discuss the diagnosis with you, the treatment required, the expected outcome and the likely number of sessions. If you need to see someone else, they will arrange the referral.

02

Treatment

Hands-on manual therapy to correct the movement problem and allow healing to start.

Treatment centres on manual therapy — mobilising and manipulating joints, nerves and muscles — to correct the postural and movement problems behind your symptoms and support early healing. You may be asked to do simple exercises at home or at work to hold those corrections between sessions.

03

Rehabilitation and prevention

A specific exercise programme so the problem does not come back.

We address the factors that contributed to the problem in the first place. Your physiotherapist designs a specific exercise programme to improve muscle function, which may include our Pilates classes, run by specialist physiotherapists. Where it is relevant you will also get training and office ergonomics advice. The aim is for you to manage the condition independently.

Plate 02 · First visit

Your first appointment

What to expect, and what to bring.

Your first appointment is an assessment. Your physiotherapist will ask you about the history of the problem — when it started, what makes it better and worse, what you need to get back to — and then examine you physically.

At the end of it you will be told the diagnosis, what treatment is required, the expected outcome, and the likely number of sessions. If physiotherapy is not the answer, we will say so and arrange a referral to the relevant specialist. That is the third promise and we mean it.

What to bring. Any scans, X-rays or reports you already have; a GP or consultant referral letter if you have one; your health insurance details; a list of any medication you take; and, if your problem is running-related, your usual running shoes. Wear or bring loose clothing you can move in — shorts are useful for lower-limb problems.

Cancellations

Please give at least 24 hours’ notice to avoid a €50 cancellation fee.

Referral

Self-referral welcome. We also accept GP and consultant referrals, and provide a consultant service to other physiotherapists.

Next step

Book an appointment, or ask us first.

You do not need a GP referral to see a chartered physiotherapist. If you are not sure whether physiotherapy is right for your problem, call — we would rather tell you now than take an appointment you do not need.
