Where does it hurt?

15 body regions, written by the physiotherapists who treat them. Pick the one closest to your symptoms to understand what is likely going on, what usually causes it, and what treatment involves.

Head and neck

  • 01 Neck Neck pain, stiffness, headaches, pain or pins and needles into the shoulder or arm →
  • 02 Jaw Jaw pain, clicking or difficulty opening the mouth, often alongside neck trouble →
  • 03 Headaches from the neck Headaches that start at the base of the skull and spread into the head →

Upper limb

  • 04 Shoulder Shoulder pain, weakness, pain down the outer arm, or difficulty lifting the arm overhead →
  • 05 Elbow Pain around the elbow or forearm when gripping, lifting or carrying →
  • 06 Wrist and hand Wrist or hand pain, often linked to keyboard work or to a fall →

Mid back

  • 07 Mid back Pain and stiffness between the shoulder blades, often posture-related →
  • 08 Ribs Sharp rib pain on movement, coughing or taking a deep breath →

Lower back and hip

  • 09 Lower back Lower back pain, with or without pain into the buttock or leg (sciatica) →
  • 10 Pelvis Pain in the lower back, buttock or groin, including pelvic pain in pregnancy →
  • 11 Hip Pain in the buttock, groin or inner thigh, or stiffness in the hip →

Lower limb

  • 12 Hamstring Pain at the back of the thigh, usually after sprinting or changing direction →
  • 13 Knee Knee pain, swelling, giving way or locking — or pain at the front of the kneecap →
  • 14 Shin Pain along the shin that comes on with running →
  • 15 Foot and ankle Ankle sprains, heel pain, fallen arches or recurrent giving way →

Not sure which?

Ring the clinic and describe it in your own words. Reception will match you to the right physiotherapist — or tell you if we are not the right people.

This is not a diagnosis

These pages explain what we commonly see. They cannot tell you what is wrong with you — that takes an assessment.

When not to wait for physiotherapy

Some symptoms need urgent medical assessment rather than physiotherapy — including loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the groin or inner thighs, weakness in both legs, severe pain following a significant injury, or pain alongside fever or unexplained weight loss. Contact your GP or an emergency department first.

If you are not sure, call us on (01) 628 0855 and we will tell you honestly whether we are the right people to see.
