Knee and patellofemoral joint

Knee

Knee problems divide into those affecting the knee joint itself and those affecting the patellofemoral joint at the front.

Contact sport can cause medial or lateral collateral ligament sprains, meniscal tears and cruciate ligament tears. A cruciate tear typically produces immediate swelling.

Patellofemoral or anterior knee pain is felt at the front of the kneecap and is typically provoked by running, by stairs and by prolonged driving. It is often linked to misalignment of the patella, to muscle imbalance, or to foot biomechanics such as overpronation.

What it probably isn’t. A knee that swelled within an hour of the injury is a different problem from one that ached and stiffened over the next day or two. Immediate swelling points to bleeding inside the joint, which is typical of a cruciate ligament tear, and should be assessed promptly.

When not to wait for physiotherapy

Some symptoms need urgent medical assessment rather than physiotherapy — including loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the groin or inner thighs, weakness in both legs, severe pain following a significant injury, or pain alongside fever or unexplained weight loss. Contact your GP or an emergency department first.

What happens if you book

Every appointment starts with assessment, not treatment. Your physiotherapist takes a history, examines you, and then tells you what is wrong, what treatment is needed and roughly how many sessions it should take.
