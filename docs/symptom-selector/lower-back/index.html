Lower back

The lower back is one of the most common sites of pain we see, and posture and lifestyle are usually part of the cause.

Pain may stay local to the lower back, or spread into the buttock and down the leg — the pattern commonly called sciatica. Pins and needles, numbness or weakness in the leg suggest greater involvement of the nerve and change how urgently you should be seen.

Common sources are the discs, the facet joints and irritated nerve roots. Slippage of one vertebra on another — spondylolisthesis — can follow trauma or repetitive injury.

Weakness in the deep core muscles, particularly transversus abdominis and gluteus maximus, is a frequent contributor and a frequent reason for recurrence.

What it probably isn’t. Pain that stays in your back is not sciatica. Sciatica means pain travelling down the leg. If your pain stops at the buttock it is more likely to be coming from the back itself — the discs or the facet joints — than from an irritated nerve root.

When not to wait for physiotherapy

Some symptoms need urgent medical assessment rather than physiotherapy — including loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the groin or inner thighs, weakness in both legs, severe pain following a significant injury, or pain alongside fever or unexplained weight loss. Contact your GP or an emergency department first.

What happens if you book

Every appointment starts with assessment, not treatment. Your physiotherapist takes a history, examines you, and then tells you what is wrong, what treatment is needed and roughly how many sessions it should take.
